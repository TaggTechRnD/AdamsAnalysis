Structural Equation Modeling (SEM)

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Structural equation modeling jointly represents relations among observed and potentially latent variables, including measurement and structural paths.

Example research questions

Does a hypothesized model link social support, stress, and measured health outcomes?

Design requirements

Identifiable measurement and structural specification, appropriately measured variables, adequate sample information, and defensible estimation assumptions.

Strengths

Integrates latent constructs, measurement error, and multiple paths in one analysis.

Limitations

Many models can fit similar data; fit statistics are not proof of causality or construct validity.

When not to use it

Do not treat excellent global fit as confirmation that all individual pathways are correct or causal.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Test a conceptual model linking latent distress, perceived support, and health-service use.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

latent structural model

\(\boldsymbol{\eta}=B\boldsymbol{\eta}+\Gamma\boldsymbol{\xi}+\boldsymbol{\zeta},\qquad \boldsymbol{y}=\Lambda_y\boldsymbol{\eta}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\)

\boldsymbol{\eta}=B\boldsymbol{\eta}+\Gamma\boldsymbol{\xi}+\boldsymbol{\zeta},\qquad \boldsymbol{y}=\Lambda_y\boldsymbol{\eta}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}

Symbols

η: endogenous latent variables; ξ: exogenous latent variables; B,Γ: structural paths; Λ_y: measurement loadings.

Explanation

SEM connects latent measurement models to a system of regression-like structural paths.

Important scope limitation

This is one standard linear SEM formulation; identification, model assumptions and competing models remain essential.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Measurement and structural relations are specified, identified and estimable with a suitable estimator and adequate sample information.

Diagnostics to perform

Check convergence, parameter identification, factor loadings, residuals, CFI/TLI, RMSEA and SRMR, plus sensitivity to alternative structures.

Consequences of violations

Weak identification or misspecification can lead to unstable parameters and invalid substantive interpretations.

Diagnostic caveats

Fit cutoffs are context dependent and should not be treated as universal proof of validity or causal identification.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Measurement loadings, structural coefficients, latent covariances and model fit indices.

Inference and tests

Parameter intervals/tests depend on estimator and identification; model chi-square addresses exact covariance structure under assumptions.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report standardized and unstandardized effects, intervals, and indirect effects where appropriate.

Model fit and performance

Use χ², degrees of freedom, CFI/TLI, RMSEA with interval and SRMR; assess local fit and alternative plausible models.

Interpreting results

A structural path quantifies the modeled relationship between latent/observed variables given the specified measurement structure.

Common mistakes

Fit indices are not probabilities the theory is true; the same covariance structure may be produced by competing models.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

SEM: χ²([df]) = [stat], CFI = [CFI], RMSEA = [RMSEA], SRMR = [SRMR]; path β = [beta] (CI [limits]).

R tutorial

Learning goal

Fit a structural relationship between two measured latent constructs.

Simulated data

Six observed survey items generated from two correlated latent factors.

Packages

lavaan

R example

set.seed(2026)
n <- 240
f1 <- rnorm(n); f2 <- 0.3*f1 + rnorm(n)
d <- data.frame(
  a1=0.9*f1+rnorm(n,sd=.5), a2=0.8*f1+rnorm(n,sd=.6),
  a3=0.7*f1+rnorm(n,sd=.6), b1=0.9*f2+rnorm(n,sd=.5),
  b2=0.8*f2+rnorm(n,sd=.6), b3=0.7*f2+rnorm(n,sd=.6))

model <- 'F1 =~ a1+a2+a3
F2 =~ b1+b2+b3
F2 ~ F1'
fit <- lavaan::sem(model,data=d,std.lv=TRUE)
print(lavaan::fitMeasures(fit,c("cfi","rmsea","srmr")))
print(lavaan::standardizedSolution(fit))

Output to inspect

Latent regression slope, standardized loadings and fit indices.

Limitations and checks

Equivalent covariance patterns can have different causal explanations.

Packages

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
n = 240
f1 = rng.normal(size=n); f2 = .3*f1 + rng.normal(size=n)
d = pd.DataFrame({
 "a1":.9*f1+rng.normal(0,.5,n), "a2":.8*f1+rng.normal(0,.6,n),
 "a3":.7*f1+rng.normal(0,.6,n), "b1":.9*f2+rng.normal(0,.5,n),
 "b2":.8*f2+rng.normal(0,.6,n), "b3":.7*f2+rng.normal(0,.6,n)})

# Install optional dependency beforehand: pip install semopy
from semopy import Model, calc_stats
model="F1 =~ a1+a2+a3\nF2 =~ b1+b2+b3\nF2 ~ F1"
fit=Model(model)
fit.fit(d)
print(calc_stats(fit).loc[["Value"],["CFI","RMSEA"]])
print(fit.inspect())
