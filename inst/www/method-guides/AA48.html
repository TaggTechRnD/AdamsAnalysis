Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE)

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Generalized estimating equations estimate population-average associations while allowing correlation within repeated or clustered observations.

Example research questions

Does treatment affect average symptom prevalence across repeated visits?

Design requirements

Repeated or clustered outcomes, appropriate mean model and link, identifiable clusters, and enough independent clusters for reliable sandwich inference.

Strengths

Focuses on marginal effects and can be robust to misspecification of the working correlation with adequate clusters.

Limitations

Small numbers of clusters can yield misleading uncertainty estimates; missing data mechanisms matter.

When not to use it

Do not interpret marginal coefficients as subject-specific effects or assume robustness solves informative missingness.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Model repeated binary adherence measurements with patient-level clustering.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

estimating equation

\(\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_i^\top V_i^{-1}(\boldsymbol{Y}_i-\boldsymbol{\mu}_i)=0\)
View LaTeX source
\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_i^\top V_i^{-1}(\boldsymbol{Y}_i-\boldsymbol{\mu}_i)=0

Symbols

i: cluster; μ_i: marginal mean vector; D_i: derivative of μ_i with respect to β; V_i: working covariance.

Explanation

GEE estimates population-average regression effects while accounting for within-cluster correlation.

Important scope limitation

A robust sandwich SE and sufficient independent clusters are important; the working correlation may be misspecified.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Clusters are independent across units, correlated outcomes within clusters are correctly assigned, and mean/link model is suitable; robust sandwich inference needs sufficient independent clusters.

Diagnostics to perform

Inspect cluster size distribution, working correlation estimates, residuals and dispersion; check the number of independent clusters and sensitivity to correlation structure.

Consequences of violations

Few clusters can make sandwich standard errors unreliable; wrong mean model biases marginal effects.

Diagnostic caveats

GEE estimates population-averaged associations; robust SE does not repair wrong mean models or confounding.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Population-average regression coefficients, working-correlation estimates and robust/sandwich standard errors.

Inference and tests

Wald tests and confidence intervals are commonly based on cluster-robust variance; small-cluster corrections may be needed.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report marginal effect as mean difference, OR or IRR according to chosen link, with cluster-aware interval.

Model fit and performance

Assess cluster counts, marginal mean specification and residuals; QIC may compare GEE models, not ordinary AIC by default.

Interpreting results

A GEE logit OR summarizes a population-average odds contrast, different from a subject-specific mixed-model OR.

Common mistakes

Working correlation need not be exactly correct for robust large-cluster inference, but few clusters can invalidate simple SEs.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

GEE population-average [effect] = [estimate] (95% robust CI [limits]); clusters = [n].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Estimate population-average effects for clustered binary outcomes.

Simulated data

280 independent binary outcomes associated with a continuous risk factor and binary treatment.

Packages

geepack

R example

set.seed(2026)
n <- 280
x <- rnorm(n)
treatment <- rbinom(n, 1, 0.5)
p <- plogis(-0.5 + 0.8*x - 0.7*treatment)
y <- rbinom(n, 1, p)
d <- data.frame(y, x, treatment)

set.seed(2027)
d$id<-rep(seq_len(70),each=4)
d$y<-rbinom(nrow(d),1,plogis(-.3+.7*d$x+rnorm(70)[d$id]*.3))
fit<-geepack::geeglm(y~x+treatment,id=id,data=d,
                     family=binomial(),corstr="exchangeable")
print(summary(fit))

Output to inspect

Marginal coefficients and robust uncertainty.

Limitations and checks

GEE requires sufficient independent clusters; working correlation is a modeling choice.

Python tutorial

Learning goal

Estimate population-average effects for clustered binary outcomes.

Simulated data

280 independent binary outcomes associated with a continuous risk factor and binary treatment.

Packages

numpy, pandas, statsmodels

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
n = 280
x = rng.normal(size=n)
treatment = rng.binomial(1, .5, n)
p = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-(-.5 + .8*x - .7*treatment)))
y = rng.binomial(1, p)
d = pd.DataFrame({"y": y,"x": x,"treatment":treatment})

import statsmodels.api as sm
from statsmodels.genmod.cov_struct import Exchangeable
d["id"]=np.repeat(np.arange(70),4)
u=rng.normal(size=70)
p=1/(1+np.exp(-(-.3+.7*d.x.to_numpy()+.3*u[d.id.to_numpy()])))
d["y"]=rng.binomial(1,p)
fit=sm.GEE.from_formula("y ~ x + treatment",groups="id",data=d,
                        family=sm.families.Binomial(),cov_struct=Exchangeable()).fit()
print(fit.summary())
