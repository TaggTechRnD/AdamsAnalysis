Paired t-Test

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

The paired t-test assesses whether the mean within-pair difference in a numeric outcome is zero.

Example research questions

Did participants' average blood pressure change between baseline and follow-up?

Design requirements

Two linked observations per subject or valid matched pair; pairs must be independent of one another and their differences should support mean-based inference.

Strengths

Uses the within-person comparison and avoids treating paired observations as independent.

Limitations

Cannot analyze complex longitudinal trends or more than two linked measurements in its simple form.

When not to use it

Do not use on unpaired groups or mistake a significant change for a treatment effect without a comparison design.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Compare each person's resting heart rate before and after a training program.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

test statistic

\(t=\frac{\bar{d}}{s_d/\sqrt{n}},\qquad d_i=y_{i,2}-y_{i,1}\)
View LaTeX source
t=\frac{\bar{d}}{s_d/\sqrt{n}},\qquad d_i=y_{i,2}-y_{i,1}

Symbols

d_i: within-pair difference; d-bar: mean difference; s_d: SD of differences; n: paired observations.

Explanation

Tests whether the mean within-person or matched-pair change equals zero.

Important scope limitation

Inference concerns the distribution of differences, not normality of each raw measurement.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Each participant contributes a valid matched pair; differences between pairs are independent. Approximate normality is needed for classical small-sample t inference on pair differences.

Diagnostics to perform

Compute within-pair differences, plot their histogram and Q-Q plot, inspect extreme differences, and verify pair identifiers.

Consequences of violations

Incorrect pairing or dependent pairs invalidates uncertainty estimates; skewed differences and outliers can distort small-sample tests.

Diagnostic caveats

It is the distribution of paired differences, not each raw measurement, that is relevant to the normality check.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Mean within-person difference, SD of paired differences, t statistic and degrees of freedom.

Inference and tests

Paired t test evaluates whether the mean paired difference is zero.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report paired mean difference with confidence interval; optionally standardized paired effect size with its definition.

Model fit and performance

Check matched identity and distribution of differences; no model fit index.

Interpreting results

A negative post-minus-pre difference indicates an average decrease after the later measurement.

Common mistakes

Do not treat the two observations as independent or interpret a before–after change as causal without design support.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

Mean paired (post − pre) change = [diff] [units] (95% CI [lower], [upper]); t([df]) = [t], p = [p].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Evaluate within-person mean change.

Simulated data

90 participants with correlated baseline and follow-up blood-pressure readings.

Packages

stats

R example

set.seed(2026)
n <- 90
before <- rnorm(n, mean = 125, sd = 13)
after <- before - 5 + rnorm(n, sd = 7)
d <- data.frame(id = seq_len(n), before, after)

print(t.test(d$after,d$before,paired=TRUE))

Output to inspect

Mean paired difference, CI, t statistic.

Limitations and checks

Check distribution of paired differences, not separate marginal normality.

Simulated data

Packages

numpy, pandas, scipy

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
n = 90
before = rng.normal(125, 13, n)
after = before - 5 + rng.normal(0, 7, n)
d = pd.DataFrame({"id": np.arange(n), "before": before, "after": after})

from scipy import stats
print(stats.ttest_rel(d.after,d.before))
print("Mean change:",(d.after-d.before).mean())
