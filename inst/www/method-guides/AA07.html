Welch's t-Test

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Welch's t-test compares two independent group means while allowing their variances to differ.

Example research questions

Do average biomarker levels differ between two clinics with unequal variability?

Design requirements

Independent groups with quantitative outcomes; distribution shape and influential values should be assessed, particularly with small groups.

Strengths

More robust than the pooled t-test to unequal variances and sample sizes.

Limitations

Still compares means and can be sensitive to extreme observations or heavy skew in small samples.

When not to use it

Do not use for paired observations or interpret the result as a test of equal medians.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Compare average recovery time across two unrelated hospitals with different standard deviations.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

test statistic

\(t_W=\frac{\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{s_1^2/n_1+s_2^2/n_2}}\)
View LaTeX source
t_W=\frac{\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{s_1^2/n_1+s_2^2/n_2}}

Symbols

x-bar_g: group mean; s_g²: sample variance; n_g: group size.

Explanation

Tests the difference between two independent means without requiring equal population variances.

Important scope limitation

Uses Welch–Satterthwaite approximate degrees of freedom; independence still matters.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Groups contain independent sampling units; outcome is continuous. Welch inference allows unequal group variances but remains sensitive to severe skew and outliers in small samples.

Diagnostics to perform

Inspect each group with boxplots and Q-Q plots, consider sample sizes and influential values, and check independence by study design.

Consequences of violations

Severe skew, extreme observations, or dependence can undermine the reference t approximation and misleadingly narrow intervals.

Diagnostic caveats

Welch does not require equal group variances. Larger samples improve robustness but do not correct selection bias or dependence.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Two means, Welch t statistic and Satterthwaite-adjusted degrees of freedom.

Inference and tests

Welch test provides a p-value for a group-mean difference without assuming equal group variances.

Effect sizes and intervals

Unpooled mean difference and confidence interval are central; standardized differences require a declared denominator.

Model fit and performance

Inspect independent sampling, outliers and distribution; no conventional model-fit statistic.

Interpreting results

The group difference is interpreted in outcome units, with inference robust to unequal variances under appropriate sampling.

Common mistakes

Welch does not fix dependence, severe distribution problems in very small samples, or confounding.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

Welch mean difference = [diff] [units] (95% CI [lower], [upper]); t([df]) = [t], p = [p].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Compare independent means without requiring equal variances.

Simulated data

160 independent observations with a continuous outcome, continuous covariate, and two groups.

Packages

stats

R example

set.seed(2026)
n <- 160
x <- rnorm(n)
group <- factor(rep(c("Control", "Treatment"), each = n/2))
y <- 10 + 1.3 * (group == "Treatment") + 0.9 * x + rnorm(n, sd = 2)
d <- data.frame(y, x, group)

print(t.test(y ~ group, data=d, var.equal=FALSE))

Output to inspect

Welch t statistic, approximate df and p-value.

Limitations and checks

Independence and suitable outcome scale remain necessary.

Python tutorial

Learning goal

Compare independent means without requiring equal variances.

Simulated data

160 independent observations with a continuous outcome, continuous covariate, and two groups.

Packages

numpy, pandas, scipy

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
n = 160
x = rng.normal(size=n)
group = np.repeat(["Control", "Treatment"], n // 2)
y = 10 + 1.3 * (group == "Treatment") + 0.9 * x + rng.normal(scale=2, size=n)
d = pd.DataFrame({"y": y, "x": x, "group": group})

from scipy import stats
u=d.loc[d.group=="Control","y"]
v=d.loc[d.group=="Treatment","y"]
print(stats.ttest_ind(u,v,equal_var=False))
