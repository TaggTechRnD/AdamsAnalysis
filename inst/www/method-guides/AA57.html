ARIMA

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Autoregressive integrated moving average models serial dependence and differencing in a univariate time series to describe patterns and forecast future values.

Example research questions

Can monthly hospital admissions be forecast using their past behavior?

Design requirements

Regular ordered observations with sufficient historical depth, appropriate differencing, and reasonably stable modeled dependence.

Strengths

Uses temporal autocorrelation systematically and can generate uncertainty intervals for forecasts.

Limitations

Structural breaks, changing seasonality, or omitted explanatory drivers can degrade forecasts; nonstationary residuals are problematic.

When not to use it

Do not randomly split time series into training and validation samples or assume past patterns survive policy changes.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Forecast monthly respiratory admissions using several years of historical counts.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

time-series model

\(\phi(B)(1-B)^d y_t=c+\theta(B)\varepsilon_t\)
View LaTeX source
\phi(B)(1-B)^d y_t=c+\theta(B)\varepsilon_t

Symbols

B: lag operator; d: differencing order; φ and θ: autoregressive and moving-average lag polynomials.

Explanation

ARIMA describes dependence in a time series after differencing where needed.

Important scope limitation

Stationarity and invertibility conditions apply to the modeled stationary component; seasonal variants extend this equation.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Time observations are ordered at a stable frequency; differenced model is approximately stationary as required and residual innovations are adequately uncorrelated for the fitted ARIMA.

Diagnostics to perform

Plot series and ACF/PACF, review differencing and seasonality, inspect residual ACF and Ljung-Box statistics, and use rolling-origin forecast tests.

Consequences of violations

Unremoved trend/seasonality or residual autocorrelation signals misspecification and unreliable intervals/forecasts.

Diagnostic caveats

Stationarity applies to the modeled transformed or differenced process, not necessarily the original observed series.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Estimated ARIMA orders (p,d,q), coefficients, residuals and forecasts with prediction intervals.

Inference and tests

Coefficient intervals may be available; forecast evaluation is more useful than treating individual term p-values as primary conclusions.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report forecast point estimates and interval bands, horizon and uncertainty assumptions.

Model fit and performance

Evaluate rolling-origin RMSE/MAE/MASE and residual autocorrelation (e.g., Ljung–Box); use AICc for suitable candidate order comparisons.

Interpreting results

Forecast intervals widen when uncertainty accumulates across longer horizons under the assumed stochastic model.

Common mistakes

A low in-sample AIC does not guarantee future forecasting accuracy, especially across structural breaks.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

ARIMA([p],[d],[q]) produced [h]-step forecast [value] (95% PI [lower], [upper]); rolling MAE = [MAE].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Fit a seasonal ARIMA model and forecast six periods.

Simulated data

180 consecutive monthly observations with trend, seasonality and serial dependence.

Packages

stats

R example

set.seed(2026)
n<-180
t<-seq_len(n)
y<-20 + .04*t + 2*sin(2*pi*t/12) + as.numeric(arima.sim(list(ar=.5),n=n,sd=1.5))
d<-data.frame(t,y)

series<-ts(d$y,frequency=12)
fit<-arima(series,order=c(1,1,1),seasonal=list(order=c(0,1,1),period=12))
print(fit)
print(predict(fit,n.ahead=6)$pred)

Output to inspect

Model coefficients, forecast and residual autocorrelation.

Limitations and checks

Choose order based on diagnostics, not arbitrary default; avoid look-ahead leakage.

Python tutorial

Learning goal

Fit a seasonal ARIMA model and forecast six periods.

Simulated data

180 consecutive monthly observations with trend, seasonality and serial dependence.

Packages

numpy, pandas, statsmodels

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng=np.random.default_rng(2026)
n=180
t=np.arange(1,n+1)
eps=rng.normal(0,1.5,n)
noise=np.zeros(n)
for j in range(1,n): noise[j]=.5*noise[j-1]+eps[j]
y=20+.04*t+2*np.sin(2*np.pi*t/12)+noise
d=pd.DataFrame({"t":t,"y":y})

from statsmodels.tsa.statespace.sarimax import SARIMAX
fit=SARIMAX(d.y,order=(1,1,1),seasonal_order=(0,1,1,12),
            enforce_stationarity=False,enforce_invertibility=False).fit(disp=False)
print(fit.forecast(steps=6))
