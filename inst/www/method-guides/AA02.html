Median and Interquartile Range

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Median and interquartile range describe the central observation and the spread of the middle half of an ordered distribution.

Example research questions

What is the typical household income and how widely do the middle 50 percent of incomes vary?

Design requirements

Ordered or numeric observations; a clearly specified sample and consistent handling of missing values.

Strengths

Resistant to extreme values and useful for skewed distributions.

Limitations

Does not describe tail behavior or show how many observations are concentrated at particular values.

When not to use it

Do not interpret the interquartile range as a confidence interval for the median or as the full data range.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Report median hospitalization cost and its interquartile range in a skewed cost dataset.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

descriptive statistic

\(\mathrm{IQR}=Q_{0.75}-Q_{0.25},\qquad \mathrm{Median}=Q_{0.50}\)
View LaTeX source
\mathrm{IQR}=Q_{0.75}-Q_{0.25},\qquad \mathrm{Median}=Q_{0.50}

Symbols

Q_p: sample quantile at probability p.

Explanation

The median is the halfway quantile; the IQR covers the middle half of observations.

Important scope limitation

Exact sample quantiles depend on the chosen interpolation convention.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Measurements are meaningfully ordered. Quantile calculation, missing-data handling, and the target population are specified.

Diagnostics to perform

Inspect frequency distributions, missing values, ties, and extreme or censored values. Check that the sample and any survey weights match the target population.

Consequences of violations

Unrepresentative sampling biases population quantiles; high concentrations of ties or censoring limit interpretability of interpolated quartiles.

Diagnostic caveats

Median and IQR describe a sample without requiring normality; they are not confidence intervals or causal estimates.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Median, 25th and 75th percentiles (Q1 and Q3), interquartile range (IQR) and sample size.

Inference and tests

Descriptive only; a separate inferential procedure is required for a hypothesis test or confidence interval for the population median.

Effect sizes and intervals

IQR = Q3 − Q1 describes the middle 50% of measurements; confidence intervals for the median need a separate procedure.

Model fit and performance

Review skew, outliers, missing values and ties; conventional model fit statistics do not apply.

Interpreting results

A median of $50 describes the midpoint; Q1–Q3 of $30–$90 describes the spread of the central half.

Common mistakes

An IQR is neither the full observed range nor a confidence interval around the median.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

Median [measure] was [median] (Q1–Q3: [Q1]–[Q3]; n = [n]).

R tutorial

Learning goal

Summarize the center and spread robustly.

Simulated data

160 independent observations with a continuous outcome, continuous covariate, and two groups.

Packages

stats

R example

set.seed(2026)
n <- 160
x <- rnorm(n)
group <- factor(rep(c("Control", "Treatment"), each = n/2))
y <- 10 + 1.3 * (group == "Treatment") + 0.9 * x + rnorm(n, sd = 2)
d <- data.frame(y, x, group)

print(quantile(d$y,c(.25,.5,.75)))
print(IQR(d$y))

Output to inspect

Median, 25th and 75th percentiles; IQR = Q3 − Q1.

Limitations and checks

Quartiles are sample descriptions, not confidence limits.

Python tutorial

Learning goal

Summarize the center and spread robustly.

Simulated data

160 independent observations with a continuous outcome, continuous covariate, and two groups.

Packages

numpy, pandas, scipy

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
n = 160
x = rng.normal(size=n)
group = np.repeat(["Control", "Treatment"], n // 2)
y = 10 + 1.3 * (group == "Treatment") + 0.9 * x + rng.normal(scale=2, size=n)
d = pd.DataFrame({"y": y, "x": x, "group": group})

print(d.y.quantile([.25,.5,.75]))
print("IQR:", d.y.quantile(.75)-d.y.quantile(.25))
