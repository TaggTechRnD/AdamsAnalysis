Difference-in-Differences (DiD)

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Difference-in-Differences estimates how an outcome changes in a treated group relative to how it changes in a comparison group around an intervention.

Example research questions

Did a policy change outcomes more in the affected region than in a comparison region?

Design requirements

Treated and comparison groups observed before and after an intervention; a defensible counterfactual trend assumption and attention to contemporaneous shocks.

Strengths

Removes common time changes and stable group differences under its identifying assumptions.

Limitations

Parallel trends are not guaranteed or testable from a single pre-intervention observation; heterogeneous or staggered treatment timing requires suitable estimators.

When not to use it

Do not interpret a significant group-by-time interaction causally without a credible comparison and identification strategy.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Compare changes in injury rates before and after a safety policy between adopting and non-adopting municipalities.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

causal contrast

\(\widehat{\mathrm{DiD}}=(\bar{Y}_{T,post}-\bar{Y}_{T,pre})-(\bar{Y}_{C,post}-\bar{Y}_{C,pre})\)
View LaTeX source
\widehat{\mathrm{DiD}}=(\bar{Y}_{T,post}-\bar{Y}_{T,pre})-(\bar{Y}_{C,post}-\bar{Y}_{C,pre})

Symbols

T,C: treated and comparison groups; pre,post: periods; Y-bar: corresponding mean outcome.

Explanation

Subtracts the comparison group’s time change from the treated group’s time change.

Important scope limitation

Causal interpretation requires a credible parallel-trends counterfactual and no major confounding shocks.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Treated and comparison units have meaningfully comparable untreated outcome trends (parallel trends), no anticipation, and no unaccounted time-varying cointerventions that differentially affect groups.

Diagnostics to perform

Graph pre-intervention trends across multiple periods when available, examine pre-trend placebo or event-study estimates, composition changes, concurrent policies and potential spillovers.

Consequences of violations

Differential preexisting trends, changing group composition or simultaneous interventions can make the DiD contrast noncausal.

Diagnostic caveats

With only one pre-intervention period, parallel trends cannot be empirically checked. Nonsignificant pretrend tests never prove the assumption.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Treatment × post-period interaction estimate, standard error and group-period mean contrasts.

Inference and tests

A confidence interval and p-value for the DiD interaction quantify statistical uncertainty, conditional on identification assumptions.

Effect sizes and intervals

DiD estimate in outcome units is the treated-group change minus comparison-group change; report a confidence interval.

Model fit and performance

Inspect pre-treatment trends, anticipation and composition; robust/clustered standard errors where warranted; R² is not causal validity.

Interpreting results

Estimate −3 means the treated group fell by three more outcome units than the comparison group over the period.

Common mistakes

Parallel trends cannot be established from a single baseline and post period; significance does not establish causal identification.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

DiD = [estimate] [units] (95% CI [lower], [upper]; p = [p]); parallel-trends evidence = [assessment].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Estimate treated-minus-control difference in pre/post changes.

Simulated data

Two groups observed before/after a policy; simulated common background trend and treatment interaction.

Packages

lmtest, sandwich

R example

set.seed(2026)
n<-160
d<-expand.grid(id=seq_len(n),post=0:1)
d$treated<-as.integer(d$id>n/2)
individual<-rnorm(n,sd=2)
d$y<-10 + individual[d$id] + 1.5*d$post + 2*d$treated*d$post + rnorm(nrow(d))

# Repeated observations: cluster-robust standard errors by id
fit<-lm(y~treated*post,data=d)
print(lmtest::coeftest(fit,vcov.=sandwich::vcovCL(fit,cluster=d$id)))

Output to inspect

Coefficient on treated:post and cluster-robust uncertainty.

Limitations and checks

Two observed periods cannot test pre-treatment parallel trends; this illustration ASSUMES parallel trends.

Python tutorial

Learning goal

Estimate treated-minus-control difference in pre/post changes.

Simulated data

Two groups observed before/after a policy; simulated common background trend and treatment interaction.

Packages

numpy, pandas, statsmodels

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng=np.random.default_rng(2026)
n=160
d=pd.MultiIndex.from_product([range(n),[0,1]],names=["id","post"]).to_frame(index=False)
d["treated"]=(d.id>=n//2).astype(int)
individual=rng.normal(0,2,n)
d["y"]=10+individual[d.id.to_numpy()]+1.5*d.post+2*d.treated*d.post+rng.normal(size=len(d))

import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
fit=smf.ols("y ~ treated*post",data=d).fit(
    cov_type="cluster",cov_kwds={"groups":d.id})
print(fit.summary().tables[1])
