Welch's ANOVA

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Welch's ANOVA compares means across three or more independent groups without assuming equal variances.

Example research questions

Do average symptom scores differ among four clinics with unequal score variability?

Design requirements

Continuous outcomes in independent groups; group mean comparisons remain meaningful and sampling is appropriate.

Strengths

More robust than conventional pooled-variance ANOVA to heteroscedasticity.

Limitations

Still sensitive to extreme skew or outliers in small groups, and requires suitable post-hoc procedures.

When not to use it

Do not treat it as a repeated-measures model or pair every group using ordinary pooled-variance post-hoc tests.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Compare average rehabilitation times among four hospitals with unequal variances.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

weighted ANOVA construction

\(w_j=\frac{n_j}{s_j^2},\qquad \bar{x}_w=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_j\bar{x}_j}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_j}\)
View LaTeX source
w_j=\frac{n_j}{s_j^2},\qquad \bar{x}_w=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_j\bar{x}_j}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_j}

Symbols

w_j: inverse-variance weight; n_j: group size; s_j²: group variance; x-bar_j: group mean.

Explanation

Welch ANOVA compares group means using precision-weighted group estimates.

Important scope limitation

These are weights and the pooled weighted mean, not the complete Welch F test; its test statistic uses an additional degrees-of-freedom correction.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Independent groups with a continuous outcome. Welch ANOVA allows heterogeneous group variances but still needs suitable within-group sampling behavior.

Diagnostics to perform

Inspect group boxplots and Q-Q plots, group sizes, outliers and independence; compare within-group spreads.

Consequences of violations

Strong outliers, severe small-group skew or dependence can distort Welch F inference.

Diagnostic caveats

Welch ANOVA does not require equal variances; omnibus significance does not identify which groups differ.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Welch heteroscedastic omnibus F statistic, approximate degrees of freedom and group summaries.

Inference and tests

Tests equality of independent-group means allowing unequal variances; use suitable unequal-variance post-hoc contrasts.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report group mean differences with heteroscedastic confidence intervals and appropriate standardized effect when useful.

Model fit and performance

Inspect independence, small cell sizes and outliers; no stand-alone predictive fit measure.

Interpreting results

A significant Welch F implies at least one mean is different, without specifying which.

Common mistakes

Welch ANOVA does not fix non-independence or identify specific group differences without contrasts.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

Welch F([df1], [df2]) = [F], p = [p]; unequal-variance post-hoc method = [method].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Compare three group means under unequal variances.

Simulated data

Two independent categorical factors with an interaction on a continuous outcome.

Packages

stats

R example

set.seed(2026)
d <- expand.grid(group = factor(c("Control","Treatment")),
                 dose = factor(c("Low","High")), rep = seq_len(40))
d$y <- 10 + 2*(d$group == "Treatment") + 1*(d$dose == "High") +
  2*(d$group == "Treatment")*(d$dose == "High") + rnorm(nrow(d), sd = 2)

# Deliberately create unequal variances across groups
z<-data.frame(y=c(rnorm(40,0,1),rnorm(40,1,3),rnorm(40,2,5)),
              grp=factor(rep(c("A","B","C"),each=40)))
print(oneway.test(y~grp,data=z,var.equal=FALSE))

Output to inspect

Welch ANOVA F and approximate degrees of freedom.

Limitations and checks

Follow-up comparisons must also handle unequal variances.

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
d = pd.MultiIndex.from_product([["Control","Treatment"],["Low","High"],range(40)],
                               names=["group","dose","rep"]).to_frame(index=False)
d["y"] = (10 + 2*(d.group == "Treatment") + (d.dose == "High") +
          2*(d.group == "Treatment")*(d.dose == "High") + rng.normal(0, 2, len(d)))

from scipy import stats
rng=np.random.default_rng(77)
groups=[rng.normal(0,1,40),rng.normal(1,3,40),rng.normal(2,5,40)]
print(stats.f_oneway(*groups,equal_var=False))
