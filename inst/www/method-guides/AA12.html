Two-Way ANOVA

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Two-way ANOVA assesses mean differences associated with two categorical factors and their interaction.

Example research questions

Does the effect of a rehabilitation program differ between age categories?

Design requirements

Continuous outcome with two factors and independent observations; sufficient combinations of factor levels for estimating effects.

Strengths

Examines both factors together and tests whether their association with the outcome depends on the other factor.

Limitations

Sparse or empty factor combinations and unequal cell sizes complicate interpretation; repeated observations need another model.

When not to use it

Do not interpret main effects without considering a substantive interaction or treat repeated observations as independent.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Compare mean fitness by treatment assignment and sex, including their interaction.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

linear model

\(Y_{ijk}=\mu+\alpha_i+\beta_j+(\alpha\beta)_{ij}+\varepsilon_{ijk}\)

Y_{ijk}=\mu+\alpha_i+\beta_j+(\alpha\beta)_{ij}+\varepsilon_{ijk}

Symbols

i,j: levels of two categorical factors; k: observation; α,β: main effects; (αβ): interaction.

Explanation

Separates differences attributable to two factors and their interaction.

Important scope limitation

Interpret main effects carefully when the interaction is present; balanced designs simplify interpretation.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Two categorical factors define valid cells with independent observations, continuous outcome, reasonable cell sizes, and adequate residual model fit; classical tests assume roughly equal residual variances.

Diagnostics to perform

Inspect design-cell counts, factor combinations, interaction plots, residual-versus-fit and Q-Q plots, and cell-specific spreads.

Consequences of violations

Empty or sparse cells can make effects nonestimable; severe variance inequality or dependent observations can invalidate conventional F tests.

Diagnostic caveats

Interactions should be examined before interpreting marginal main effects. Factor coding and unbalanced designs affect interpretation.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

F statistics for two factors and their interaction, degrees of freedom and estimated marginal means.

Inference and tests

Separate tests assess each main effect and interaction; contrasts and multiplicity adjustments address detailed comparisons.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report interaction contrasts with confidence intervals; η² or partial η² with definition.

Model fit and performance

Inspect model residuals, variance behavior and cell sizes; consider unbalanced-design sums-of-squares conventions.

Interpreting results

An interaction indicates the effect of one factor differs across levels of the other.

Common mistakes

Do not interpret an average main effect without considering a meaningful interaction or confuse association with causal effect.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

Factor A × B interaction: F([df1], [df2]) = [F], p = [p], partial η² = [effect].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Estimate main and interaction effects of two between-person factors.

Simulated data

Two independent categorical factors with an interaction on a continuous outcome.

Packages

stats

R example

set.seed(2026)
d <- expand.grid(group = factor(c("Control","Treatment")),
                 dose = factor(c("Low","High")), rep = seq_len(40))
d$y <- 10 + 2*(d$group == "Treatment") + 1*(d$dose == "High") +
  2*(d$group == "Treatment")*(d$dose == "High") + rnorm(nrow(d), sd = 2)

fit<-aov(y ~ group*dose,data=d)
print(summary(fit))

Output to inspect

Group-by-dose interaction F test.

Limitations and checks

Interpret interaction before interpreting marginal main effects.

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
d = pd.MultiIndex.from_product([["Control","Treatment"],["Low","High"],range(40)],
                               names=["group","dose","rep"]).to_frame(index=False)
d["y"] = (10 + 2*(d.group == "Treatment") + (d.dose == "High") +
          2*(d.group == "Treatment")*(d.dose == "High") + rng.normal(0, 2, len(d)))

import statsmodels.api as sm
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
fit=smf.ols("y ~ C(group)*C(dose)",data=d).fit()
print(sm.stats.anova_lm(fit,typ=2))
