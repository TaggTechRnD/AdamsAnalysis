One-Way ANOVA

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

One-way ANOVA tests whether mean outcomes vary across levels of one independent categorical factor.

Example research questions

Do average cholesterol levels differ among three dietary groups?

Design requirements

Quantitative outcome and independent groups; residual distribution and comparable within-group variability should support the model.

Strengths

Compares several group means in a unified framework rather than repeated pairwise tests.

Limitations

A global test does not identify which groups differ; unbalanced variability may require another approach.

When not to use it

Do not use for repeated measurements or infer which pair differs solely from the omnibus p-value.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Compare average reaction time across three independent sleep-condition groups.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

test statistic

\(F=\frac{MS_{\mathrm{between}}}{MS_{\mathrm{within}}}\)
View LaTeX source
F=\frac{MS_{\mathrm{between}}}{MS_{\mathrm{within}}}

Symbols

MS_between: between-group mean square; MS_within: pooled within-group mean square.

Explanation

Tests whether all group population means can be treated as equal in a one-factor model.

Important scope limitation

Standard one-way ANOVA assumes independent errors and homogeneous variances; F itself does not identify which groups differ.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Independent observations across groups with continuous outcome; roughly normal within-group errors and similar group variances for classical F inference.

Diagnostics to perform

Inspect residuals versus fits and Q-Q plots, group SDs and outliers; assess unequal cell sizes and independence of sampling units.

Consequences of violations

Heterogeneous variances combined with unequal sizes can distort F tests; strong departures and influential points affect results.

Diagnostic caveats

Normality concerns residuals rather than all pooled data. Welch ANOVA may suit markedly unequal variances.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Between-group F statistic, numerator and denominator degrees of freedom, group means and omnibus p-value.

Inference and tests

Omnibus F test evaluates equality of all group means; specific pairwise claims require appropriate contrasts and multiplicity handling.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report group mean differences with intervals and η² or ω² with a clear denominator.

Model fit and performance

Examine model residuals and variance heterogeneity; R² or explained-variance fraction may be reported for equivalent linear models.

Interpreting results

A significant overall F suggests at least one group mean differs, not which pairs differ.

Common mistakes

An omnibus p-value is not proof that every group differs or that the group factor caused the differences.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

One-way ANOVA: F([df1], [df2]) = [F], p = [p], ω² = [omega2]; contrasts were [method].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Compare means across independent group levels.

Simulated data

Two independent categorical factors with an interaction on a continuous outcome.

Packages

stats

R example

set.seed(2026)
d <- expand.grid(group = factor(c("Control","Treatment")),
                 dose = factor(c("Low","High")), rep = seq_len(40))
d$y <- 10 + 2*(d$group == "Treatment") + 1*(d$dose == "High") +
  2*(d$group == "Treatment")*(d$dose == "High") + rnorm(nrow(d), sd = 2)

fit<-aov(y ~ group,data=d)
print(summary(fit))

Output to inspect

Between-group F statistic and p-value.

Limitations and checks

Check independent units, variance pattern, and residuals.

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
d = pd.MultiIndex.from_product([["Control","Treatment"],["Low","High"],range(40)],
                               names=["group","dose","rep"]).to_frame(index=False)
d["y"] = (10 + 2*(d.group == "Treatment") + (d.dose == "High") +
          2*(d.group == "Treatment")*(d.dose == "High") + rng.normal(0, 2, len(d)))

import statsmodels.api as sm
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
fit=smf.ols("y ~ C(group)",data=d).fit()
print(sm.stats.anova_lm(fit,typ=2))
