Repeated-Measures ANOVA

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Repeated-measures ANOVA evaluates changes in a continuous outcome across conditions or time measured in the same participants.

Example research questions

Does mean fatigue change over three scheduled assessments of the same participants?

Design requirements

The same participants measured at multiple conditions or visits, with an appropriate within-subject factor and suitable repeated-measures assumptions.

Strengths

Accounts for the paired nature of repeated measures in a comparatively simple design.

Limitations

Missing visits and unequal measurement schedules are difficult to accommodate; sphericity is relevant with three or more levels.

When not to use it

Do not use as a substitute for a model of complex clusters or assume sphericity is always satisfied.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Compare physical performance in the same participants at baseline, week four, and week eight.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

repeated-measures model

\(Y_{it}=\mu+\tau_t+b_i+\varepsilon_{it}\)
View LaTeX source
Y_{it}=\mu+\tau_t+b_i+\varepsilon_{it}

Symbols

i: participant; t: repeated-measure level; τ_t: within-person factor effect; b_i: subject effect.

Explanation

Represents repeated observations on the same individual and compares within-person means.

Important scope limitation

This is a schematic representation; classical repeated-measures ANOVA also imposes covariance requirements (including sphericity for >2 levels).

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Repeated outcomes are linked by participant identifiers and independent across participants. Classical repeated-measures ANOVA assumes appropriate within-person covariance; sphericity matters with at least three within-subject levels.

Diagnostics to perform

Check visit counts and missingness, residual Q-Q plots, subject trajectories, and Mauchly or epsilon estimates for sphericity when within-subject factor has more than two levels.

Consequences of violations

Unmodeled missing visits or within-person covariance can distort standard errors and F tests. Sphericity violations require suitable corrections or other models.

Diagnostic caveats

With exactly two repeated levels, sphericity is automatic. Mauchly test does not establish all assumptions.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Within-subject factor F statistic, degrees of freedom, estimated means and subject-level repeated changes.

Inference and tests

Tests repeated-measure level effects; adjusted degrees of freedom (e.g., Greenhouse–Geisser) may be needed for >2 levels.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report pairwise within-person contrasts with intervals and generalized/partial η² with clear definition.

Model fit and performance

Examine repeated-measure covariance assumptions; sphericity is automatic with exactly two repeated levels.

Interpreting results

A significant time effect means at least one measurement occasion differs in average outcome.

Common mistakes

A time effect does not demonstrate treatment causality, and sphericity correction does not repair arbitrary missingness.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

Repeated-measures ANOVA: F([df1], [df2]) = [F], p = [p] ([correction]); time contrast = [diff] [units].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Test changes over repeated visits ignoring between-group effects.

Simulated data

90 participants randomized to two groups, each measured at three visits (long format).

Packages

stats

R example

set.seed(2026)
n <- 90
d <- expand.grid(id = seq_len(n), time = factor(c("Baseline","Week6","Week12"),
                 levels=c("Baseline","Week6","Week12")))
d$group <- factor(ifelse(d$id <= n/2, "Control", "Treatment"))
subject_effect <- rnorm(n, sd = 3)
d$y <- 30 + subject_effect[d$id] + 1.2*(as.integer(d$time)-1) +
  2*(d$group == "Treatment")*(as.integer(d$time)-1) + rnorm(nrow(d), sd=2)

d$id<-factor(d$id)
fit<-aov(y ~ time + Error(id/time),data=d)
print(summary(fit))

Output to inspect

Within-person time F statistic.

Limitations and checks

For between-group effects or missing visits use mixed ANOVA/mixed model; sphericity matters with >2 visits.

Learning goal

Simulated data

Packages

numpy, pandas, scipy

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
n = 90
d = pd.MultiIndex.from_product([range(n), ["Baseline","Week6","Week12"]],
                               names=["id","time"]).to_frame(index=False)
d["group"] = np.where(d.id < n // 2, "Control", "Treatment")
subject_effect = rng.normal(0, 3, n)
t = d.time.map({"Baseline": 0, "Week6": 1, "Week12": 2}).to_numpy()
d["y"] = (30 + subject_effect[d.id.to_numpy()] + 1.2*t +
          2*(d.group.to_numpy() == "Treatment")*t + rng.normal(0,2,len(d)))

from statsmodels.stats.anova import AnovaRM
print(AnovaRM(data=d,depvar="y",subject="id",within=["time"]).fit())
