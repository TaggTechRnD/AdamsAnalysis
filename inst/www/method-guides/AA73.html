Distributed Lag Non-Linear Model (DLNM)

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Distributed lag non-linear models represent how an exposure-response association varies jointly by exposure level and time lag, allowing cumulative delayed effects.

Example research questions

How do high temperatures affect hospital admissions over the current day and subsequent weeks?

Design requirements

Time-ordered exposure and outcome data, a scientifically justified lag window, sufficient exposure variation, and adjustment for relevant temporal confounding and dependence.

Strengths

Captures nonlinear and delayed associations in one interpretable exposure-lag framework.

Limitations

Lag specification and smoothing choices influence results; collinearity across lagged exposures and temporal confounding complicate causal claims.

When not to use it

Do not infer causal delayed effects from an unadjusted time-series association or choose lag windows purely by the strongest significance.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Estimate immediate and delayed temperature associations with daily respiratory admissions over lags zero through fourteen days.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

cross-basis model term

\(\eta_t=\alpha+\sum_{j,k}\beta_{jk}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}B_j(x_{t-\ell})C_k(\ell)+\boldsymbol{z}_t^\top\boldsymbol{\gamma}\)

\eta_t=\alpha+\sum_{j,k}\beta_{jk}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}B_j(x_{t-\ell})C_k(\ell)+\boldsymbol{z}_t^\top\boldsymbol{\gamma}

Symbols

η_t: linear predictor; x_t-ℓ: historical exposure; B_j: exposure basis; C_k: lag basis; L: maximum lag; z_t: adjustment variables.

Explanation

DLNMs express a potentially nonlinear exposure–response association distributed over historical lags.

Important scope limitation

This is a cross-basis term within a larger model; the response distribution, lag range, confounding, and temporal structure require separate specification.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Exposure precedes the modeled response lags; lag window, exposure-response basis, time trend and confounders are prespecified or justified; temporal dependence and outcome family are suitably modeled.

Diagnostics to perform

Inspect exposure and outcome time alignment, plausible lag ranges, seasonal/trend controls, basis dimensions, concurvity, residual ACF and sensitivity to lag/basis choices.

Consequences of violations

Time misalignment, undercontrolled seasonality or poorly chosen lag bases produce spurious cumulative or delayed associations.

Diagnostic caveats

DLNM coefficients and cumulative effects are sensitive to basis specification; lagged association does not by itself identify causality.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Exposure–response–lag surface, individual-lag and cumulative association estimates, model predictions.

Inference and tests

Pointwise and cumulative confidence intervals/tests depend on basis specification and time-series dependence handling.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report relative risk or outcome difference at specified exposures and lags, with cumulative effect and 95% CI.

Model fit and performance

Assess basis choice, maximum lag, residual autocorrelation and robustness to temporal confounding; AIC only compares specified models.

Interpreting results

A cumulative relative risk of 1.2 at a specified exposure compares modeled accumulated risk over the defined lag window with a reference exposure.

Common mistakes

Do not interpret multiple lag-specific peaks as independent effects or assume observed exposure–lag associations are causal.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

For [exposure] versus [reference] over lags 0–[L], cumulative [RR/difference] = [estimate] (95% CI [limits]).

R tutorial

Learning goal

Demonstrate a nonlinear temperature association distributed over several lags.

Simulated data

180 consecutive monthly observations with trend, seasonality and serial dependence.

Packages

dlnm

R example

set.seed(2026)
n<-180
t<-seq_len(n)
y<-20 + .04*t + 2*sin(2*pi*t/12) + as.numeric(arima.sim(list(ar=.5),n=n,sd=1.5))
d<-data.frame(t,y)

set.seed(2027)
n<-nrow(d)
temp<-20+8*sin(2*pi*d$t/30)+rnorm(n,sd=2)
lag1<-c(NA,temp[-n])
d$y<-5+.08*(temp-20)^2+.05*(lag1-20)^2+rnorm(n)
cb<-dlnm::crossbasis(temp,lag=3,
       argvar=list(fun="bs",df=4),arglag=list(fun="ns",df=3))
fit<-glm(d$y~cb+d$t,family=gaussian())
print(summary(fit)$coefficients)

Limitations and checks

Python example uses separate lag splines, NOT a smooth lag-axis crossbasis; neither example implements a full confounding-adjusted epidemiological DLNM.

Python tutorial

Learning goal

Demonstrate a nonlinear temperature association distributed over several lags.

Simulated data

180 consecutive monthly observations with trend, seasonality and serial dependence.

Packages

numpy, pandas, patsy, statsmodels

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng=np.random.default_rng(2026)
n=180
t=np.arange(1,n+1)
eps=rng.normal(0,1.5,n)
noise=np.zeros(n)
for j in range(1,n): noise[j]=.5*noise[j-1]+eps[j]
y=20+.04*t+2*np.sin(2*np.pi*t/12)+noise
d=pd.DataFrame({"t":t,"y":y})

# Python approximation: exposure spline by individual lag, not exact dlnm crossbasis
from patsy import dmatrix
import statsmodels.api as sm
n=len(d)
temp=20+8*np.sin(2*np.pi*d.t.to_numpy()/30)+rng.normal(0,2,n)
lag1=np.r_[np.nan,temp[:-1]]
y=5+.08*(temp-20)**2+.05*(lag1-20)**2+rng.normal(size=n)
L=np.column_stack([np.roll(temp,j) for j in range(4)])[3:]
B=dmatrix("bs(z, df=4, degree=3, include_intercept=False) - 1",
          {"z":L.ravel()},return_type="dataframe").to_numpy()
features=B.reshape(len(L),4,4).reshape(len(L),16)
X=sm.add_constant(np.column_stack([features,d.t.to_numpy()[3:]]))
fit=sm.OLS(y[3:],X).fit()
print("Spline model R-squared:",fit.rsquared)

Implementation notes

analog: Python lag-spline approximation, not a direct DLNM equivalent
