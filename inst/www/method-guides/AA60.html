Propensity Score Methods

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Propensity score methods use the estimated probability of treatment assignment given observed pretreatment covariates to improve comparability between observational groups.

Example research questions

Can outcomes be compared between treated and untreated patients with similar observed baseline characteristics?

Design requirements

Observational treatment indicator, relevant pretreatment covariates, adequate overlap, and a credible no-unmeasured-confounding assumption for causal claims.

Strengths

Offers matching, weighting, or stratification strategies that make measured covariate balance assessable.

Limitations

Cannot eliminate unmeasured confounding; extreme weights and poor overlap can make estimates unstable.

When not to use it

Do not treat a balanced propensity score as proof of randomized equivalence or adjust for post-treatment variables.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Weight treated and untreated rehabilitation patients by their estimated treatment propensities before comparing outcomes.

e(x)=P(T=1\mid X=x),\quad \widehat{ATE}_{IPW}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i\left(\frac{T_iY_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{(1-T_i)Y_i}{1-e(X_i)}\right)

Symbols

e(x): propensity score; T: treatment indicator; Y: outcome; ATE_IPW: inverse-probability-weighted effect.

Explanation

A propensity score summarizes measured treatment-assignment predictors; inverse weighting targets a treatment contrast.

Important scope limitation

This is one estimator, not all propensity-score methods; unmeasured confounding, positivity and score estimation matter.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Treatment assignment is conditionally exchangeable given measured pre-treatment covariates, with positivity/overlap and a defensible treatment model.

Diagnostics to perform

Plot propensity score overlap, inspect standardized mean differences after weighting/matching, extreme weights, effective sample size, and covariate missingness.

Consequences of violations

Poor overlap and unstable weights inflate variance or change the estimand; unmeasured confounding biases estimated treatment effects.

Diagnostic caveats

Balance diagnostics cannot establish absence of unmeasured confounding. Removing nonoverlap may change the target population.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Propensity scores, matching/weighting diagnostics, covariate balance, treatment-effect estimate.

Inference and tests

Inference for ATT/ATE depends on estimator, overlap, weighting/matching uncertainty and outcome model.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report target estimand (ATE or ATT), effect in outcome units or risk scale, and CI using appropriate robust/resampling variance.

Model fit and performance

Standardized mean differences, weight distributions, effective sample size and overlap are central; propensity model AUC alone is not enough.

Interpreting results

After acceptable covariate balance, the estimated contrast compares treatment groups on the chosen estimand scale.

Common mistakes

Observed covariate balance cannot rule out unmeasured confounding; propensity score quality is not judged by classification accuracy.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

[ATE/ATT] = [estimate] [units] (95% CI [limits]); max absolute standardized difference after adjustment = [value].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Estimate inverse-probability-weighted treatment difference using measured covariate x.

Simulated data

Observational treatment assignment dependent on measured confounder x; continuous outcome.

Packages

lmtest, sandwich

R example

set.seed(2026)
n<-450
x<-rnorm(n)
p_treat<-plogis(-.2+.9*x)
treated<-rbinom(n,1,p_treat)
y<-4+1.6*treated+1.4*x+rnorm(n,sd=1.2)
d<-data.frame(y,x,treated)

psfit<-glm(treated~x,family=binomial(),data=d)
ps<-pmin(pmax(predict(psfit,type="response"),.02),.98)
w<-ifelse(d$treated==1,1/ps,1/(1-ps))
fit<-lm(y~treated,data=d,weights=w)
print(lmtest::coeftest(fit,vcov.=sandwich::vcovHC(fit,type="HC1")))
print(tapply(w,d$treated,summary))

Output to inspect

Weighted treatment coefficient, weight range and covariate balance (to check separately).

Limitations and checks

Truncation, overlap, covariate balance, missing confounders, and variance estimation require care; not automatic causal proof.

Python tutorial

Learning goal

Estimate inverse-probability-weighted treatment difference using measured covariate x.

Simulated data

Observational treatment assignment dependent on measured confounder x; continuous outcome.

Packages

numpy, pandas, statsmodels

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng=np.random.default_rng(2026)
n=450
x=rng.normal(size=n)
p_treat=1/(1+np.exp(-(-.2+.9*x)))
treated=rng.binomial(1,p_treat)
y=4+1.6*treated+1.4*x+rng.normal(0,1.2,n)
d=pd.DataFrame({"y":y,"x":x,"treated":treated})

import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
psfit=smf.logit("treated ~ x",data=d).fit(disp=False)
ps=np.clip(psfit.predict(d),.02,.98)
w=np.where(d.treated==1,1/ps,1/(1-ps))
fit=smf.wls("y ~ treated",data=d,weights=w).fit(cov_type="HC1")
print(fit.summary().tables[1])
print(pd.Series(w).describe())
