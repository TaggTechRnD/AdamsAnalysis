Hierarchical Clustering

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Hierarchical clustering builds a tree of nested groupings from pairwise dissimilarities using a selected linkage rule.

Example research questions

Do patient profiles suggest nested subgroups at different levels of similarity?

Design requirements

Meaningful dissimilarity measure, appropriate feature preparation, and enough observations to interpret a dendrogram.

Strengths

Displays clustering structure at several resolutions without fixing one final group count beforehand.

Limitations

Results depend heavily on distance and linkage choices; outliers can distort the hierarchy.

When not to use it

Do not assume every dendrogram branch corresponds to a stable or clinically meaningful subgroup.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Explore hierarchical groupings of standardized metabolic biomarkers.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

linkage criterion

\(d_{\mathrm{single}}(A,B)=\min_{i\in A,j\in B}d(\boldsymbol{x}_i,\boldsymbol{x}_j)\)
View LaTeX source
d_{\mathrm{single}}(A,B)=\min_{i\in A,j\in B}d(\boldsymbol{x}_i,\boldsymbol{x}_j)

Symbols

A,B: clusters; d: observation distance.

Explanation

Agglomerative hierarchical clustering joins clusters based on a linkage-defined distance.

Important scope limitation

Single linkage is one option; complete, average and Ward linkage use different rules.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Distance and linkage method reflect substantive similarity; preprocessing handles mixed scales, missingness and unusual observations appropriately.

Diagnostics to perform

Check distance scaling, dendrogram stability, linkage sensitivity, cophenetic correlation and resampling robustness.

Consequences of violations

Unstable distances/linkages or outliers can induce arbitrary splits and misleading dendrogram structure.

Diagnostic caveats

A dendrogram always yields branches even if the population has no natural clusters.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Dendrogram, linkage heights, cluster assignments after tree cut and cluster profiles.

Inference and tests

Standard hierarchical clustering has no automatic p-values or confidence intervals for branches.

Effect sizes and intervals

Summarize separation, cluster stability and descriptive differences, not treatment effects.

Model fit and performance

Cophenetic correlation and cluster stability may characterize hierarchy; silhouette may help choose cut level.

Interpreting results

Merge heights describe dissimilarity under the chosen distance/linkage, not significance of a biological division.

Common mistakes

Branches can shift with scaling and linkage choice; a dendrogram does not validate the number of real clusters.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

Hierarchical clustering used [distance] and [linkage]; cut at k = [k], silhouette = [value].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Create a hierarchy of clusters and cut at a specified level.

Simulated data

180 unlabeled observations from three well-separated feature clusters.

Packages

stats

R example

set.seed(2026)
X <- rbind(matrix(rnorm(120, mean=-2, sd=.5), ncol=2),
           matrix(rnorm(120, mean=2, sd=.5), ncol=2),
           cbind(rnorm(60,mean=2,sd=.5),rnorm(60,mean=-2,sd=.5)))
colnames(X)<-c("feature1","feature2")

h<-hclust(dist(scale(X)),method="ward.D2")
print(table(cutree(h,k=3)))

Output to inspect

Ward linkage merge tree and three-group partition.

Limitations and checks

Linkage and scaling affect results; dendrogram proximity is not statistical significance.

Packages

Python example

import numpy as np
rng=np.random.default_rng(2026)
X=np.vstack([rng.normal(-2,.5,(60,2)),rng.normal(2,.5,(60,2)),
             np.column_stack([rng.normal(2,.5,60),rng.normal(-2,.5,60)])])

from scipy.cluster.hierarchy import linkage,fcluster
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
Z=linkage(StandardScaler().fit_transform(X),method="ward")
print("Cluster sizes:",np.bincount(fcluster(Z,3,criterion="maxclust"))[1:])
