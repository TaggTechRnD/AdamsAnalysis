Interrupted Time Series

Statistical method reference guide

Overview and appropriate use

Purpose

Interrupted time-series analysis estimates whether the level or trend of an outcome changed after a defined policy or intervention point.

Example research questions

Did a smoke-free policy coincide with a change in monthly hospital-admission trends?

Design requirements

Numerous ordered observations before and after a clearly dated intervention, with attention to underlying trends, autocorrelation, seasonality, and coincident events.

Strengths

Uses pre-intervention trends as a counterfactual framework and can examine immediate and gradual changes.

Limitations

Without a comparison series, simultaneous external events threaten causal attribution; short time series constrain inference.

When not to use it

Do not assume a post-policy break was caused by the policy when other events changed at the same time.

Illustrative research scenario (hypothetical)

Estimate whether a new triage protocol altered monthly emergency waiting times.

Mathematical foundation

Mathematical foundation

segmented regression

\(Y_t=\beta_0+\beta_1t+\beta_2I(t\geq T)+\beta_3(t-T)I(t\geq T)+\varepsilon_t\)
View LaTeX source
Y_t=\beta_0+\beta_1t+\beta_2I(t\geq T)+\beta_3(t-T)I(t\geq T)+\varepsilon_t

Symbols

T: intervention time; β2: level shift; β3: slope change; I: indicator.

Explanation

Estimates immediate and gradual outcome changes at a specified interruption.

Important scope limitation

Confounding concurrent events, secular trends, autocorrelation and seasonality must be addressed.

Assumptions and diagnostics

Assumptions and conditions

Intervention time is defensible with enough pre/post observations; counterfactual trend/seasonality and autocorrelation are modeled and major simultaneous changes considered.

Diagnostics to perform

Graph long pre/post series, check intervention-date sensitivity, residual ACF, secular trend, seasonal structure and cointerventions or measurement changes.

Consequences of violations

Misspecified baseline trends, serial correlation or concurrent policies can create spurious interruption effects.

Diagnostic caveats

Interrupted time series provides stronger quasi-experimental evidence with multiple observations, but cannot fully rule out concurrent causes.

Understanding the results

Primary outputs

Baseline level/trend, immediate level-change coefficient and post-intervention slope-change coefficient.

Inference and tests

Intervals and tests on level and slope changes require correct serial-correlation handling and prespecified intervention timing.

Effect sizes and intervals

Report level shift and trend change with intervals; forecast counterfactual differences at meaningful times where justified.

Model fit and performance

Check pre-intervention trend, seasonality, autocorrelation, concurrent events and model residuals; AIC is not a causal validity check.

Interpreting results

A negative level-change estimate represents an immediate modeled drop at interruption; slope change describes subsequent divergence.

Common mistakes

Changes may reflect concurrent events; one or two pre-periods are insufficient for credible trend modeling.

Example report wording (placeholder values)

Immediate level change = [estimate] (95% CI [limits]); slope change = [estimate] per period; serial correlation handled by [method].

R tutorial

Learning goal

Model level and slope changes at a known intervention date.

Simulated data

180 consecutive monthly observations with trend, seasonality and serial dependence.

Packages

lmtest, sandwich

R example

set.seed(2026)
n<-180
t<-seq_len(n)
y<-20 + .04*t + 2*sin(2*pi*t/12) + as.numeric(arima.sim(list(ar=.5),n=n,sd=1.5))
d<-data.frame(t,y)

d$post<-as.integer(d$t>=100)
d$since<-pmax(0,d$t-99)
d$y<-d$y+2*d$post+.1*d$since
fit<-lm(y~t+post+since+sin(2*pi*t/12)+cos(2*pi*t/12),data=d)
print(lmtest::coeftest(fit,vcov.=sandwich::NeweyWest(fit,lag=12,prewhite=FALSE)))

Output to inspect

Post-intervention level change and change in slope.

Limitations and checks

A segmented regression alone cannot exclude contemporaneous confounding; serial correlation must be checked.

Python tutorial

Learning goal

Model level and slope changes at a known intervention date.

Simulated data

180 consecutive monthly observations with trend, seasonality and serial dependence.

Packages

numpy, pandas, statsmodels

Python example

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
rng=np.random.default_rng(2026)
n=180
t=np.arange(1,n+1)
eps=rng.normal(0,1.5,n)
noise=np.zeros(n)
for j in range(1,n): noise[j]=.5*noise[j-1]+eps[j]
y=20+.04*t+2*np.sin(2*np.pi*t/12)+noise
d=pd.DataFrame({"t":t,"y":y})

import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
d["post"]=(d.t>=100).astype(int)
d["since"]=np.maximum(0,d.t-99)
d["y"]+=2*d.post+.1*d.since
fit=smf.ols("y ~ t + post + since + np.sin(2*np.pi*t/12) + np.cos(2*np.pi*t/12)",data=d).fit(
    cov_type="HAC",cov_kwds={"maxlags":12})
print(fit.summary().tables[1])
